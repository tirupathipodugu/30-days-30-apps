How the numbers work

An illustration from a simple energy-balance model, not a prediction or medical advice.

  • What you burn: the Mifflin–St Jeor estimate of resting energy, times your activity. Without your details it assumes 30–44, 170 cm, 72 kg and some walking.
  • What you eat: the foods on your plate, with typical portions.
  • Weight change follows a simplified version of Hall's dynamic model (Hall et al., The Lancet, 2011): in the long run about 1 kg for every 24 kcal a day over or under, with about half of it showing in the first year. kg(t) = (surplus ÷ 24) × (1 − 0.5^(t ÷ 365 days))
  • The face stops changing beyond +15 kg and never goes leaner than −8 kg.
  • Time ages everyone a little. Fine lines come in from about 5 years and a little faster with alcohol, sugar or smoking. Grey at the temples starts at 10 years.
  • The better plate swaps the two or three heaviest treats for lighter ones and stops once your day is in balance.

Everything runs on this device. Nothing is uploaded or stored. For adults 18+.
